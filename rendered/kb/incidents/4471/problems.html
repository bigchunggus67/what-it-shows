The three problems

parser: the shared parser reads dates month first. Fixed. Our tenants write the day first, so a booking for 7 April came out as 4 July. Every channel showed it: app, email, and booking-v2’s form. This is the bug #4471 was opened for.

hint: the booking-v3 form asks for the wrong order. Won’t fix: the form is retired. Its date field prints MM/DD/YYYY under it. People at day-first tenants type what the hint says, month first, and the form reads the date their tenant’s way, day first. The result looks the same as the parser bug, a day and month swapped, but it only comes in through the booking-v3 form, and the parser fix does not touch it. Retiring the form ends it.

one-day-move: dates move one day earlier. Open. These reports do not swap day and month. A Tuesday shows as the Monday before; an invite arrives before the booking was made; the same meeting lands on Tuesday for a client in Lisbon and Monday for one in Toronto. That points at time zone handling, not at date order. The desk had already noted these reports as a one-day move, but the cause table counts them under parser by tenant, so they had no ticket. The desk still counts them there, and so does the table above.

Why one ticket hid them

  • Merging on subject (decision 0001) puts every “wrong date” report in one ticket, whatever its cause. The subjects of all three problems match.
  • Intake’s tag is a guess from the reporter’s browser. A US browser at a European tenant gets tagged hint; a European browser on the booking-v3 form gets tagged parser. Counting tags gives the wrong split. The cause comes from the tenant: its locale, its booking form and the channel.
  • The export repeats reports. The ticket system’s pages overlap, so some reports appear twice. Count by report id.

For the desk

  • Don’t take #4471 being closed to mean every date report is solved. Check that the one-day move reports stop before closing its ticket.
  • Merging on subject alone will do this again. A decision record that replaces 0001 should merge on cause (tenant, form and channel), not subject.
